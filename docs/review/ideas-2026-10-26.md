# Ideas for the next level (2026-10-26)

Asked by the user after the F-Droid source landed: "Har du noen flere gode ideer for nå virkelig ta dette oppsettet til
neste nivå?" Ideas only: nothing here is approved, built or queued. Checked against PLAN.md (the queue, Parked, "Asked for,
scope not settled"), `docs/plan/2026-10-25-queue-rounds.md` §0/§4, designs 25, 34, 35, 44, 48, 56, 59, 61-64, 66, r13
and the two Element X reviews, so nothing below repeats a queued, parked or dropped item. Where an idea extends a queued
one (B2's emergency menu, the Immich wallpaper) it says so.

The yardstick, from the owner's standing rules: privacy first and no loopholes (no cloud, no third-party analytics, the
phone never serves, the server owns the rules); calm and adult but for kids, never attention-seeking; every restriction
liftable by the parent; no adb for any feature; Element X stays; Spotify stays parked; music follows vibb; docker
compose stays the whole install. One more value read from the code: **the kid is told** (Kid Settings' "Telefonen deler
hvor den er med mamma og pappa", the rule card, the time left) - several ideas below push that further.

Each idea: what it gives, why it fits, what it builds on, size, the main risk, and how it could be abused or leak.

## A. For the kid - a first phone, not a cage

**A1. "Mine regler": the phone explains its own rules** (S, launcher only)
A calm page in Kid Settings that reads the cached policy back in the kid's words: "Skjermtid: 1 t 30 min på hverdager",
"Leggetid: 21:00-07:00 (fredag og lørdag 22:30)", "Skole: 08:15-14:30, bare nødanrop", "Du kan ringe: Mamma, Pappa,
Mormor", "Telefonen deler hvor den er" - and nothing the kid can change. Rules a kid can read are rules a kid can argue
about at the dinner table instead of discovering them as a lock; it is the opposite of a cage that surprises.
- Builds on: `KidSettingsActivity` (reads only an `Ok` cache), `timerules/TimeRules.kt`, design 66's bedtime, the
  `kid_settings_location_shared` line, `CallPolicyStore`. No contract change: the phone already holds all of it.
- Risk: wording a rule set with exceptions and lifts simply; show the plan, not today's lifts.
- Abuse/leak: nothing leaves the phone; the page shows only what the kid already lives under (no PIN, no parent data).

**A2. "Be om ...": a contact and an app join "Be om mer tid"** (M, both dirs + a contract file)
Today the kid can ask for more time (design r13, `POST /api/devices/time-request`, the anchor's "Be om mer tid"). Add
two more asks: "Legg til en venn" (a name and a number, typed once by the kid) and "Ønsk en app" (picked from the
catalog's apps not checked for this phone). The parent gets the inbox row and push, answers Yes/No in the PWA, and a Yes
lands as an ordinary contact or app switch. It removes the parent's most common chore (typing a friend's number from a
text message) and gives the kid a legitimate path instead of a workaround.
- Builds on: `server/testdata/time_request.json`'s request/answer shape, `Kind::ExtraTimeRequest`, the PWA's family
  address book (one entry per number), `device_tracked_apps`.
- Risk: the catalog list on the phone is a new policy field (both dirs, `policy_json_keys_snapshot`); a rate limit.
- Abuse/leak: nothing is callable or installed before the parent's Yes; the request carries a name and a number only.

**A3. A heads-up before a rule or the budget starts** (XS-S, launcher only)
Ten minutes before a locking rule or the end of the daily budget, the anchor notification's title (already "1 t 20 min
igjen i dag", r13) reads "Leggetid om 10 min" / "Skjermtiden slutter om 10 min", and the glance's rule card shows
"Leggetid kl. 21:00" from 30 minutes before. No sound, no second notification (the user's "helst ikke" to persistent
extras is kept: it is the same one notification). A lock that never surprises is the calmest lock.
- Builds on: `TimeRuleAlarm`'s next boundary, `lock/RuleCard.kt`, r13's anchor title in `CommandListenerService`.
- Risk: one more exact alarm per boundary (Doze delivery was never device-tested, design 06's checklist).
- Abuse/leak: none; it tells the kid what the server already decided.

## B. For the parent - less work, more trust

**B1. "Uka": a weekly look in the PWA, not an email** (S-M, server only)
One card on a phone's summary each Sunday evening: screen time per day against the budget (a bar per day), time
requests asked and answered, zone arrivals counted (not traced), apps installed and updated, low-battery and phone-off
nights, crashes. One push "Uka til Sverre er klar" under its own Notify Me About switch, off by default. It answers "is
this working?" in ten seconds, instead of the parent reading the inbox.
- Builds on: `parent_events` (every kind is stored 90 days), design 48's Activity log, `time_state.rs` - which keeps only
  today's `used_minutes`, so a small `daily_usage` table (day, used, budget, extra) filled at the day's last report.
- Risk: it must stay a summary of what the parent already sees; no per-app minutes (see "Rejected").
- Abuse/leak: server-side only, under the same login; the push says "ready", never a number.

**B2. A phone health card** (S, both dirs + a contract file)
Android's security patch date (`Build.VERSION.SECURITY_PATCH`), free storage, battery cycle count where the phone
reports it (API 34's `EXTRA_CYCLE_COUNT`), days since the last restart, the last OS update - on About This Phone, with one
calm warning when the patch is over 90 days old or storage under 1 GB. The Jelly Star's updates are rare; the parent
should know when the phone itself becomes the weak point.
- Builds on: the status report and its contract-file pattern (`installed_apps_report.json`), design 38's boots,
  `device_about.html`, crash reports.
- Risk: OEMs report cycle counts unevenly; show "unknown" rather than guess.
- Abuse/leak: device facts only, no identifiers beyond what the report carries today.

**B3. "Overnatting": a sleepover / day-away preset with an end** (M, server + a small launcher part)
One form, one end time: the host parent's number callable until Sunday 12:00, tonight's bedtime moved to 22:30, Locate
every 30 min while it runs. At the end everything reverts by itself, logged in the Activity log. Today the parent would
edit three pages and has to remember to undo them; "hand the phone to a friend's parent" becomes "add the friend's parent
for one night".
- Builds on: lifts (time-bounded, already in the policy), the bedtime page's exceptions, contacts, `location_policy`.
- Risk: a contact with an expiry is new on both sides (`call_policy`); the rest is existing lifts.
- Abuse/leak: the temporary contact is call-only and expires on the phone even offline (the expiry travels with it).

**B4. School holidays from the skolerute** (S-M, server only)
The parent pastes the kommune's skolerute (an ICS link where one exists, else a list of dates) once a year; on those
days every school-kind rule is lifted automatically, shown as "Høstferie" on Device > Time. Today a holiday morning means
the kid wakes to "Skole · Bare nødanrop" unless the parent remembered.
- Builds on: lifts (the server raises one per holiday - no contract change), the server's existing fetch sweeps.
- Risk: which kommuner publish ICS; the date list is the fallback and the likely common case.
- Abuse/leak: the server fetches a public calendar; nothing about the family is sent.

## C. Home and family

**C1. The family calendar on the glance** (S, launcher + catalog rows)
Now that F-Droid is a source: ICSx5 (`at.bitfire.icsdroid`, read-only feeds - the school's, the football club's) and,
if the family runs one, DAVx5 (`at.bitfire.davdroid`) for a shared CalDAV calendar; Etar shows them. The glance gets one
quiet line under the alarm: "Neste: Fotball 17:30". The kid learns to run his own day from his own phone.
- Builds on: the F-Droid source and Etar (just added), the glance's line style (design 25, r9), `READ_CALENDAR`
  granted by the device owner.
- Risk: the feed URL is typed on the phone once (during a pause, no adb); DAVx5 needs an account the parent sets up.
- Abuse/leak: calendar data is read on the phone only and never reported; the glance line follows design 51's
  "show the text" choice so a lock screen can hide it.

**C2. A family room on Home: Element X's voice messages as the walkie-talkie** (S, both dirs)
A Home tile "Familien" that opens the family's Element X group room directly. Element X already records voice messages,
so "trykk og snakk" to the whole family costs no new code on the media side. One tap from Home instead of opening the
chat list.
- Builds on: `elementx://open/<session>/<room>` and the learned `ElementRoomStore` (design 15), Home tiles
  (`device_home.html`), the glance's Element X rows.
- Risk: queue decision 15 dropped the parent-set `message_room`; this reintroduces a parent-picked room id for one tile.
  Alternative: the kid pins a learned room from the glance row, no server field.
- Abuse/leak: only rooms the kid is already in; nothing about the room reaches the server beyond an id the parent typed.

**C3. "Fortsett på Stua": one chip when the last speaker is near** (S, music only)
When the kid plays on the phone and the music app's discovery sees the room he last played on, the player shows one
quiet chip: "Fortsett på Rommet til Sverre". One tap = today's hand-over. Coming home and continuing the audiobook on
the room's speaker becomes one tap, not three.
- Builds on: "Spill av på", `SpeakerCache`, `RoomScope`, the hand-over and its timing log (design 54), the parent's
  Sonos rooms allowlist on Device > Music.
- Risk: "music follows vibb" - check that vibb's own player has no contrary behaviour; never auto-switch.
- Abuse/leak: only rooms the parent allowed ("a friend's too" is already the page's warning); no new network calls.

## D. Safety

**D1. "Funnet telefonen?" on Android's lock face, and a Lost-mode message** (XS-S, both dirs)
A parent-set line on Android's own lock face - "Funnet denne telefonen? Ring 4x xx xx xx" - shown before the first
unlock and in the gaps the glance doesn't cover; Locate's Lock command may carry a one-line message ("Ring mamma") that
replaces it until unlocked. A finder sees exactly the face this line sits on.
- Builds on: `AndroidLock.lockScreenInfo` (`setDeviceOwnerLockScreenInfo`, used today only for the restart line),
  Locate's lock command, design 59's android mode.
- Risk: android mode only (own mode has no such face; phase D removes own mode anyway).
- Abuse/leak: anyone holding the phone reads it - so a parent number only, no name of the child, no medical data by
  default (an ICE line is the parent's explicit choice, with the warning).

**D2. "Er du fremme?": a check-in the parent sends, the kid answers** (S-M, both dirs + a contract file)
From the summary: "Spør om Sverre er fremme". The phone shows a calm card on the glance and over Home: "Mamma lurer på:
er du fremme?" with "Ja" and "Ring meg"; the answer comes back as an inbox event. Optionally "si fra når han er på
Skolen" - a one-shot arrival push for today only. It replaces a silent location lookup with a question the kid
answers - more trust, less surveillance.
- Builds on: `device_commands` + the SSE nudge, the time-request contract's answer shape, design 64's zone events.
- Risk: design 64 §10 lists "a 'didn't arrive by' alarm" as out of scope; the "not by 08:30" variant needs the user's yes.
- Abuse/leak: the answer carries no position unless the parent asked for "with location", and the card says so.

**D3. "Følg meg hjem": a live share the kid starts** (M, both dirs + a contract file)
From the glance or Kid Settings: "Del turen hjem". The phone sends a fix every minute for at most 60 minutes, ends by
itself on entering the Home zone, shows a visible "Du deler turen hjem · Stopp" while it runs, and the parent gets
"Sverre deler turen hjem" with Locate following live. The kid owns the start and the stop.
- Builds on: design 64's zone watch (`ZonePlan`, `PassiveFixes`, the arrival), Locate's map and trail (design 44),
  the location line in Kid Settings.
- Risk: battery on the Jelly Star (3000 mAh) at one GPS fix a minute; cap and pick balanced fixes between.
- Abuse/leak: kid-started only - the parent cannot start it remotely (that is what Update Location is for); the trail
  follows the phone's retention like every fix.

**D4. "Si fra til mamma og pappa" in the emergency menu** (S, on top of round B2)
One row under 112 in the configurable emergency menu (queued as B2): an urgent push to every parent with a fresh fix,
then a call to the first parent contact. For the moments that are scary but not 112 - lost in town, a bad situation at
a party.
- Builds on: B2's sheet (decision 12: non-emergency rows follow the call rules), `Kind::urgent()`, the
  `emergency_call` event's path.
- Risk: it must never delay or crowd 112, which stays first and largest.
- Abuse/leak: the push goes to the family's parents only; a rate limit keeps a pocket-tap from becoming ten pushes.

## E. Platform - the next family

**E1. A "Kom i gang" checklist in the PWA** (S, server only)
A card on Devices until done: 2FA set, Phone Setup (language, zone, server URL), backups target, a push test shown,
the first phone's QR, the first zone. Each row links to the page that exists; each tick is read from state, nothing
new stored. The next family gets from `docker compose up` to an enrolled phone without reading DEPLOY.md twice.
- Builds on: `install.sh`, `compose.yaml`, `provisioning_settings.html`, `provision_qr.html`, `backups.html`,
  `setup_2fa.html`, Notifications' test.
- Risk: keeping the list short (six rows); "Wi-Fi in Phone Setup" (decision 11) would be a seventh.
- Abuse/leak: none; it reads the server's own state behind the login.

**E2. "Sett opp som Anna sin": a second phone in minutes** (S-M, server only)
Device > Add gets "copy from": the catalog apps checked, Home's tiles, the web filter, the zones, the shared contacts;
rules already follow Everyone's rules. A second child's phone becomes a QR scan plus a review page.
- Builds on: `device_add.html`, `device_tracked_apps`, the family address book, Everyone's rules, zones.
- Risk: what must never copy - the child PIN, the location history, the avatar.
- Abuse/leak: copies stay within one family's server.

**E3. A demo server and a ten-minute emulator trial** (M, server + scripts)
`handy-server --demo` seeds a throwaway database from the test fixtures (a family, two phones, a week of events, fake
keys that look fake) and starts the fake Sonos/Storytel/audio scripts; `docs/testing/emulator.md` gets a "try it"
path: an AVD, the QR, done. A family deciding whether to self-host can see the whole product first.
- Builds on: `scripts/fake-*.py`, `server/testdata/`, `scripts/smoke-test.sh`, `dev-rebuild.sh`.
- Risk: a public hosted demo is an attack surface and upkeep; start local-only.
- Abuse/leak: demo data is fixture data; the flag refuses a database that holds a real phone.

## F. Wild cards (bold, clearly not for now)

**F1. The family's own Matrix homeserver in the compose file** (L)
A Tuwunel/conduwuit container next to the server; the PWA makes the kid's Matrix account, adds each contact's Matrix ID,
and the family's chats never touch matrix.org. *Why maybe not*: Element X calls need MatrixRTC (LiveKit and its JWT
service - two more containers), key backup and federation are real operations work, and "compose stays simple" bends.

**F2. The phone grows up, then lets go** (M then L)
On the kid's birthday the PWA proposes a yearly review: what to loosen (a later bedtime, a bigger budget, the browser).
At the end: a "release" flow that hands the phone to the teen - `clearDeviceOwnerApp`, apps and data kept, Vibb One an
ordinary launcher. A product that plans its own end is the strongest "not a cage" statement. *Why maybe not*: a
birthdate stored; going back means a factory reset; the release is a one-way door a stolen parent session must not reach
(step-up, design 22).

**F3. Friend links between two Vibb families** (L)
Two servers exchange a signed "friend card" (the kid's name, number, Matrix ID); both families' parents approve, and
each phone gets the other kid as a contact. *Why maybe not*: cross-server trust and a protocol to maintain, for a handful
of families; A2's typed request covers 90 % of it.

## The five I would build first

1. **D1 "Funnet telefonen?"** - XS-S, android mode is the destination, and a lost phone is the likeliest bad day.
2. **A3 the heads-up before a rule** - XS-S, removes the daily "it just locked!" friction with one alarm and no
   new notification.
3. **C1 the family calendar on the glance** - S, cashes in the F-Droid source this week; mostly catalog rows.
4. **D2 "Er du fremme?"** - S-M, the most-used safety question, answered by the kid, not by tracking.
5. **A2 "Be om ..."** - M, takes the parent's most frequent chore away and gives the kid a legitimate path.
Runners-up: A1 "Mine regler" (cheap, launcher only) and D3 "Følg meg hjem" (after the zones' Jelly Star day).
Each would go into PLAN's queue only on the user's yes, with a design note first for the both-dirs ones.

## Rejected on purpose

- **Reading the kid's messages or browsing for the parent** (keyword alerts, AI scanning): `GlanceReader` reads only
  what the parent ticked for the lock face and reports nothing; that line stays.
- **Per-app minutes and usage rankings**: one budget, not a surveillance table; B1 stays at the day level.
- **Gamified screen time** (streaks, badges, earning minutes): attention-seeking, and it turns time into a currency.
- **Chores and allowance**: scope creep into family management, money on a kid's phone, and the same currency problem.
- **Location to third parties** (Life360-style circles, friends' parents seeing the map): F3 shares a contact, never
  a position.
- **Covert anything**: a remote camera or microphone, "listen in" calls (some kid watches sell it), location without
  the kid's line.
- **A cloud relay or hosted Vibb**: the phone never serves and the family owns the server; Web Push stays the only
  third party, end-to-end encrypted.
- **Anything Spotify** (parked) and **FluffyChat** (assessed 2026-10-26: stay on Element X).
